# Guided Notes as an Instructional Strategy

## Evidence Summary (Biggers & Luo, 2020)

22 studies reviewed (2009–2019).
- 15/17 studies: improved learning outcomes
- Students universally appreciate guided notes
- Guided notes group: 10% higher quiz scores; 36% more accurate notes vs. no guided notes (Glodowski & Thompson, 2018)
- Effective across ALL modalities and content areas
- Benefits strongest with complex content; strong effects on delayed assessments (retention)
- Metacognitive prompts embedded in notes significantly boost higher-order thinking outcomes

## What Works Best

What works best: partial (skeletal) notes outperform complete notes; graphic organizers/matrices outperform linear formats; metacognitive questioning embedded in notes (e.g., "What is the main idea? How does this connect to X?"); notes provided BEFORE class (not after) improve preparation and attendance.

## Theoretical Grounding

Theoretical grounding: reduces cognitive load; supports self-regulated learning; promotes generative learning; functions as scaffolding.

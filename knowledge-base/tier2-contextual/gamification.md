# Gamification in Higher Education

## Evidence Summary (Lister, 2015)

19 studies reviewed. Common elements: points, badges/achievements, leaderboards, levels.
- 12/19 studies: positive motivation effects
- Most consistent outcome: increased attendance and participation (correlates with performance)
- Performance effects: MIXED — Hanus & Fox (2015) found lower exam scores in gamified courses vs. non-gamified

## Game Element Effects

- Points: generally motivating; instant feedback; must be combined with other elements
- Levels: strong motivator; 93% of students track them; 89% work toward achievements; provide clear published criteria
- Badges: variable — low-performers motivated by participation badges; high-performers by mastery badges; ~1/3 motivated, ~1/3 neutral, ~1/3 not motivated; allow opt-out
- Leaderboards: motivating for competitive students; demotivating for others — allow opt-out; private comparison preferred

## Design Requirements

Design requirements: narrative, scaffolding, freedom to fail, and meaningful progression are all essential. Game elements alone are insufficient. Requires significant upfront design investment — recommend instructional designer collaboration.

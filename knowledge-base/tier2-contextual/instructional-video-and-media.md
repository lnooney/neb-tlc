# Instructional Video and Media

## Evidence Summary (Noetel et al., 2021)

105 RCTs, n=7,776 students.
- Adding video supplementally: large effect (g=0.80–0.88); ~88% of implementations benefit learning
- Swapping other content for video: small effect (g=0.28); ~50% of implementations show meaningful positive effects
- Video especially effective for skill acquisition (g=0.44) over knowledge recall (g=0.18)
- CRITICAL MODERATOR — interactivity: when comparison condition offers more interactivity, video shows NO benefit
- Effects robust across disciplines, video types (recorded lectures, skill demonstrations), and course duration

## Evidence-Based Design Principles

Evidence-based design principles: coherence (remove tangential content), signaling (highlight key info), learner control/self-pacing, conversational language (informal outperforms formal narration), short chunked segments, authentic first-person demonstrations for skills.

## Practical Applications

Practical: Don't just record lectures — edit for focus. Add video to existing teaching rather than replace it. Use embedded questions (H5P, EdPuzzle, Panopto quizzing) to maintain interactivity. Blended approaches outperform either alone.
